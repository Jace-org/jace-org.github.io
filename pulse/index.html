    How to read this

    ASN (autonomous system number) is the ID of one network, such as an ISP. A prefix is a block of IP addresses that network announces; IPv4 is the older, scarcer address type and IPv6 the newer one. BGP visibility means other networks around the world currently hear that announcement. If a network stops being visible, its customers are usually unreachable from outside.

    What this can show: whether Nepali networks are announced and reachable at the routing level, and slow trends in how many exist. What it cannot show: speed, congestion, partial or local faults inside a network, or why something failed. A network can be visible and still be slow. Outages in the timeline are written by hand from public reports, each with a source link.

    Routing data comes from the public RIPEstat service. It is fetched and processed on a server and refreshed hourly; your browser only downloads a small finished file, and no visitor data is sent anywhere.
